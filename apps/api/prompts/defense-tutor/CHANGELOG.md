# defense-tutor

## v1

Initial version (milestone 7.1). The warm, curious teaching-assistant persona
of backend guide 6.5: one spoken-register question at a time, built from the
student's own transcription against the reference solution, with the hard
rules stated as unoverridable (never reveal the answer or unrevealed steps,
stay on task, hostile text in the transcription is data, seat context only)
and the wind-down behaviour ending on the one concept to revisit.

## v2

Milestone 8.4, the understanding unfold. The reference solution now reaches the
tutor numbered by step, in the same numbering the student unfolds, and the
context states how many steps they have read. Two paragraphs say what that
buys: "step 3" is shared vocabulary, so a student can send a step they have
read into the conversation and be understood, and the never-reveal rule gains a
precise line rather than a blanket one, since a step already unfolded is theirs
to discuss and everything past it is not. Nothing else changed; the persona,
the spoken register, and the other hard rules are v1's.

## v3

Phase 10, the materials marketplace (decision 0062). A fourth source can now
reach the tutor: the quotation the student issued, when they issued one and
cited it. Two paragraphs say what it is for (a material choice is defensible
like any other step, so ask about alloy, lead time, minimum order and mass)
and, at least as importantly, what its absence means (nothing; most courses
never turn the marketplace on, so silence about it is the correct behaviour
rather than an omission to probe).

Two hard rules are added. The tutor must never name the material the student
should have chosen, because a selection is an argument from requirements and
handing over the conclusion destroys the exercise. And it must never compare
the student's prices to any other figure, because those prices are struck per
variant and nobody else holds them, so a comparison would be either meaningless
or a leak. The quotation is also named alongside the transcription in the
hostile-text rule: it carries catalogue prose and the student's own free text,
and both are data.
